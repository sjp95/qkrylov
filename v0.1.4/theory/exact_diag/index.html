    Exact Diagonalization (ED)

    Exact Diagonalization (ED) is an unbiased, numerically exact method for solving quantum many-body lattice models without approximation or sign problems.

    1. The Curse of Dimensionality

    Section titled “1. The Curse of Dimensionality”

    In quantum lattice systems with NNN sites, each with a local Hilbert space of dimension ddd, the total Hilbert space dimension scales exponentially:

    D=dND = d^ND=dN

    • Spin-1/2 systems (d=2d=2d=2): D=2ND = 2^ND=2N. For N=30N=30N=30, D=230≈1.07×109D = 2^{30} \approx 1.07 \times 10^9D=230≈1.07×109.
    • Fermionic Hubbard models (d=4d=4d=4): D=4N=22ND = 4^N = 2^{2N}D=4N=22N. For N=16N=16N=16, D≈4.29×109D \approx 4.29 \times 10^9D≈4.29×109.

    Storing a full dense Hamiltonian matrix requires 16D216 D^216D2 bytes (for double-precision complex numbers). Even for a modest N=16N=16N=16 spin-1/2 system (D=65,536D = 65,536D=65,536), a dense matrix requires ~68.7 GB of RAM, while full diagonalization (O(D3)O(D^3)O(D3)) requires trillions of floating-point operations.

    2. Symmetry Reduction & Block Diagonalization

    Section titled “2. Symmetry Reduction & Block Diagonalization”

    Physical Hamiltonians often conserve total quantum numbers such as total spin projection SzS^zSz, particle number NpN_pNp​, or spatial lattice translation momentum kkk:

    [H,Sz]=0,[H,N^]=0[H, S^z] = 0, \quad [H, \hat{N}] = 0[H,Sz]=0,[H,N^]=0

    Under these commuting symmetries, the Hamiltonian block-diagonalizes into independent invariant sectors:

    H=⨁sHsH = \bigoplus_{s} H_sH=⨁s​Hs​

    For example, restricting an N=20N=20N=20 spin-1/2 Heisenberg chain to the zero-magnetization sector Sz=0S^z = 0Sz=0 reduces the dimension from 220=1,048,5762^{20} = 1,048,576220=1,048,576 to:

    DSz=0=(2010)=184,756D_{S^z=0} = \binom{20}{10} = 184,756DSz=0​=(1020​)=184,756

    an over 5.6-fold reduction in state dimension.

    3. Sparsity of Many-Body Hamiltonians

    Section titled “3. Sparsity of Many-Body Hamiltonians”

    Typical physical interactions are local (e.g. nearest-neighbor exchange J∑⟨i,j⟩S⃗i⋅S⃗jJ \sum_{\langle i, j \rangle} \vec{S}_i \cdot \vec{S}_jJ∑⟨i,j⟩​Si​⋅Sj​). Consequently, each basis state ∣n⟩|n\rangle∣n⟩ connects to only O(N)\mathcal{O}(N)O(N) other basis states under HHH.

    The sparsity ratio is:

    NonzerosD2≈zDD2=zD→0as N→∞\frac{\text{Nonzeros}}{D^2} \approx \frac{z D}{D^2} = \frac{z}{D} \to 0 \quad \text{as } N \to \inftyD2Nonzeros​≈D2zD​=Dz​→0as N→∞

    where z∝Nz \propto Nz∝N is the coordination number times number of interaction terms. In an N=24N=24N=24 system (D≈2.7×106D \approx 2.7 \times 10^6D≈2.7×106), more than 99.999%99.999\%99.999% of the Hamiltonian matrix entries are identically zero.

    4. The Matrix-Free Paradigm

    Section titled “4. The Matrix-Free Paradigm”

    Traditional sparse solvers store matrix non-zero elements using formats such as Compressed Sparse Row (CSR). However, as DDD reaches 107−10910^7 - 10^9107−109, even storing column indices and values requires tens of gigabytes of RAM before performing any calculation.

    qkrylov eliminates explicit matrix storage entirely:

    • The operator expression is represented as an OpSum.
    • The action y=Hxy = H xy=Hx is evaluated directly by applying local operator transformations to state bitstrings on-the-fly.
    • Memory consumption scales strictly as O(D)\mathcal{O}(D)O(D) (the size of the vectors), allowing researchers to push exact diagonalization to the limits of physical hardware.
