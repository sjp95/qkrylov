    Spectral Functions & Dynamical Response

    Dynamical correlation functions provide direct theoretical predictions for experimental spectroscopies, including inelastic neutron scattering (INS), resonant inelastic X-ray scattering (RIXS), and angle-resolved photoemission spectroscopy (ARPES).

    1. Zero-Temperature Green’s Functions

    Section titled “1. Zero-Temperature Green’s Functions”

    Given a Hamiltonian HHH, ground state ∣ψ0⟩|\psi_0\rangle∣ψ0​⟩, and ground-state energy E0E_0E0​, the zero-temperature retarded Green’s function for an operator O^\hat{O}O^ is:

    G(ω)=⟨ψ0∣O^†1ω+E0−H+iηO^∣ψ0⟩G(\omega) = \langle \psi_0 | \hat{O}^\dagger \frac{1}{\omega + E_0 - H + i\eta} \hat{O} | \psi_0 \rangleG(ω)=⟨ψ0​∣O^†ω+E0​−H+iη1​O^∣ψ0​⟩

    The corresponding spectral function (or dynamical structure factor) is given by:

    A(ω)=−1πIm⁡G(ω)=∑n∣⟨n∣O^∣ψ0⟩∣2η/π(ω−(En−E0))2+η2A(\omega) = -\frac{1}{\pi} \operatorname{Im} G(\omega) = \sum_{n} |\langle n | \hat{O} | \psi_0 \rangle|^2 \frac{\eta/\pi}{(\omega - (E_n - E_0))^2 + \eta^2}A(ω)=−π1​ImG(ω)=∑n​∣⟨n∣O^∣ψ0​⟩∣2(ω−(En​−E0​))2+η2η/π​

    In the limit η→0+\eta \to 0^+η→0+, this reproduces the discrete Lehmann representation ∑n∣⟨n∣O^∣ψ0⟩∣2δ(ω−(En−E0))\sum_n |\langle n | \hat{O} | \psi_0 \rangle|^2 \delta(\omega - (E_n - E_0))∑n​∣⟨n∣O^∣ψ0​⟩∣2δ(ω−(En​−E0​)). The small positive parameter η>0\eta > 0η>0 acts as a Lorentzian broadening factor corresponding to experimental finite resolution or lifetime.

    2. Continued Fraction Expansion

    Section titled “2. Continued Fraction Expansion”

    To compute G(z)G(z)G(z) where z=ω+E0+iηz = \omega + E_0 + i\etaz=ω+E0​+iη without diagonalizing the full spectrum of HHH, we initialize the Lanczos algorithm using the perturbed state:

    ∣v1⟩=O^∣ψ0⟩∥O^∣ψ0⟩∥|v_1\rangle = \frac{\hat{O} |\psi_0\rangle}{\|\hat{O} |\psi_0\rangle\|}∣v1​⟩=∥O^∣ψ0​⟩∥O^∣ψ0​⟩​

    Projecting z−Hz - Hz−H onto the resulting Krylov subspace yields the tridiagonal matrix zI−Tmz I - T_mzI−Tm​.     G(z)=⟨ϕ0∣ϕ0⟩z−α1−β12z−α2−β22z−α3−…G(z) = \frac{\langle \phi_0 | \phi_0 \rangle}{z - \alpha_1 - \cfrac{\beta_1^2}{z - \alpha_2 - \cfrac{\beta_2^2}{z - \alpha_3 - \dots}}}G(z)=z−α1​−z−α2​−z−α3​−…β22​​β12​​⟨ϕ0​∣ϕ0​⟩​

    where αn\alpha_nαn​ and βn\beta_nβn​ are the standard Lanczos recurrence coefficients.

    Key Advantages

    Section titled “Key Advantages”
    • Single Krylov Run: Once the coefficients {αn,βn}n=1M\{\alpha_n, \beta_n\}_{n=1}^M{αn​,βn​}n=1M​ are computed, G(z)G(z)G(z) and A(ω)A(\omega)A(ω) can be evaluated across thousands of frequency points ω\omegaω in milliseconds.
    • Adjustable Broadening: The broadening parameter η\etaη can be interactively varied during post-processing without re-running matrix operations.

    3. The Correction Vector Alternative

    Section titled “3. The Correction Vector Alternative”

    While continued fractions are ideal for broad frequency sweeps, high numbers of Lanczos steps (M>100M > 100M>100) can develop ghost peaks due to loss of orthogonality in the Krylov basis.

    For ultra-high resolution at specific target frequencies ω\omegaω, qkrylov also implements the correction vector method:

    (H−E0−ω−iη)∣x(ω,η)⟩=O^∣ψ0⟩(H - E_0 - \omega - i\eta) |x(\omega, \eta)\rangle = \hat{O} |\psi_0\rangle(H−E0​−ω−iη)∣x(ω,η)⟩=O^∣ψ0​⟩

    The spectral function is recovered directly via:

    A(ω)=−1πIm⁡⟨O^ψ0∣x(ω,η)⟩=ηπ∥x(ω,η)∥2A(\omega) = -\frac{1}{\pi} \operatorname{Im} \langle \hat{O} \psi_0 | x(\omega, \eta) \rangle = \frac{\eta}{\pi} \|x(\omega, \eta)\|^2A(ω)=−π1​Im⟨O^ψ0​∣x(ω,η)⟩=πη​∥x(ω,η)∥2

    This shifts the problem to an iterative linear system solve, providing certified accuracy within the residual tolerance tol.
